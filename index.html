About the book

Personal Data Breaches in Denmark (2018–2026) examines how personal data breaches have been reported in Denmark since the GDPR became applicable across the EU. It draws on 68,454 breach notifications submitted to the Danish Data Protection Agency (Datatilsynet) between 25 May 2018 and 31 May 2026.

Part I sets out the foundations: how personal data is defined, classified, and processed under the GDPR and Danish rules, and the incident types Datatilsynet uses to record breaches. It also introduces the MEO Framework, which categorises unintentional incidents as Mistakes, Errors, or Omissions.

Part II analyses the notification data: the most common incident types, the sectors and industries that report most, and how the patterns have changed over eight years. It closes with practical measures for strengthening data security.

The book is written for privacy and cybersecurity professionals, legal and compliance practitioners, data governance teams, researchers, and analysts.

Source: Datatilsynet breach statistics, data as at 6 July 2026.

Inside the book

Seven chapters in two parts. Preview chapters will be linked here as they are released.

Part I. Foundations of Personal Data Protection

  1. 1 Denmark’s Digital Society and Personal Data Fundamentals Forthcoming

    This chapter provides the foundation for understanding the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) within the Danish context. It explains the core principles of personal data processing, data classification, and levels of sensitivity. It also distinguishes the main types of personal data breaches, covers breach notification, and outlines the proposed Single-Entry Point (SEP).

  2. 2 Incident Types and Risk Categorisation Forthcoming

    This chapter explains the incident types defined by the Danish Data Protection Agency (Datatilsynet) and the risks they pose to personal data. It then introduces the MEO Framework (Mistakes, Errors, and Omissions), which categorises unintentional breaches by failure mode. Together, the incident types and the MEO Framework help organisations understand how breaches occur and identify appropriate safeguards.

  3. 3 Sectors and Industries Forthcoming

    This chapter explains how the Danish Data Protection Agency (Datatilsynet) classifies data controllers by sector and industry. It also identifies the industries with the most reported personal data breaches between 2018 and 2026.

Part II. Personal Data Breach Trends and Sector Insights

  1. 4 Dataset Overview and Insights Forthcoming

    This chapter describes the source of the dataset used to analyse personal data breaches reported in Denmark. It explains the methodology for collecting and categorising the data to examine trends across sectors, industries, and incident types. It also addresses the dataset's limitations and their implications for interpreting the findings.

  2. 5 Summary of Findings Forthcoming

    This chapter summarises the key findings, highlighting major trends and recurring patterns in personal data breaches reported in Denmark. It identifies the most common incident types and the sectors with the most notifications, and provides an overview before the detailed analysis in Chapter 6.

  3. 6 Breach Patterns: Analysis and Interpretation Forthcoming

    This chapter examines the dataset across sectors and years, exploring the practical implications of the findings. It applies the MEO Framework to examine patterns of Mistakes, Errors, and Omissions in reported unintentional breaches. The results help organisations prioritise their data security measures.

  4. 7 Breach Insights and Measures: Strengthening Data Security Forthcoming

    This chapter brings together insights from earlier chapters to present a clear approach to protecting personal data throughout its lifecycle. It draws on the MEO Framework to explain why Mistakes, Errors, and Omissions require different safeguards. The chapter connects prevention, breach response, and organisational learning to help organisations strengthen data security in everyday practice.
